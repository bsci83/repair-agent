# Safety warnings — Detroit Diesel Series 60

> **This is a teaching corpus, not a substitute for the OEM service
> manual.** Always follow the lockout/tagout, eye-protection, and
> hot-component procedures in the official DDC documentation and your
> shop's safety policies.

## Hot coolant pressure

The cooling system on a Series 60 holds pressure at 15-18 psi when
hot. Opening the surge tank or radiator cap on a hot engine can
scald. Let the engine cool to below 100°F before opening any coolant
cap, or use the slow-release technique on the surge cap and stand
clear of the steam path.

If a coolant cap must be opened hot to diagnose a boil-over, do it
through a heavy rag and stand to the side. The water and antifreeze
in the system flash to steam and burn skin on contact.

## Hot EGR

The EGR cooler, valve, and surrounding exhaust plumbing reach
temperatures that ignite grease on contact and burn exposed skin
within a second. Let the engine sit for at least 30 minutes after a
hard run before removing any EGR component. Wear leather gloves,
not latex — latex melts on hot metal and the molten glove is worse
than the burn alone.

The EGR cooler also holds soot and condensed water. Removing it cold
is fine; removing it warm can crack the casting from uneven contraction.

## Hot fuel pressure

The fuel system on a DDEC Series 60 holds residual pressure in the
secondary lines and the injector return rails even after shutdown.
Cracking a fuel line at the filter head will spray diesel at skin
temperature; a fuel injector that fires under load is at full
cylinder temperature in its tip.

Open fuel fittings sparingly under load, and only with the engine
stopped and the keys out of the cab. Capture fuel in a clean pan,
not on the shop floor.

## Electrical hazards

The DDEC ECM and the engine wiring harness carry low-voltage signals
that are sensitive to static discharge. Don't ground-test with the
ECM connected; pull the connector before resistance checks, and use
an ESD-safe mat when handling the ECM itself.

The starter motor and the alternator both carry full battery voltage
at full current. Remove rings, watches, and conductive bracelets
before working around the starter.
